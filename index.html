C:\PROJECTS\QUANTUM\QECLAT

QEC Decoder Latency StudyReal-Time Quantum Error Correction Against a Coherence Deadline

Jun 2026 – Oct 2026COMPLETEDINDEPENDENT RESEARCH

Can a classical decoder written in ordinary software close the quantum error-correction loop (measure the syndrome, decode, apply the correction) before superconducting qubits decohere? I implemented the 3-qubit bit-flip code with real-time feed-forward in Qiskit, timed 10,000 decode cycles each for a Python decoder and a compiled C decoder, and measured how often each missed a 40 µs per-round deadline (T₂ = 120 µs over three syndrome rounds).

KEY RESULTS

  • Typical decodes took 113 ns in Python and 31 ns in C, 350 and 1,300 times inside the 40 µs budget; C was 3.6× faster at the median.
  • At 40 µs, Python missed once in 110,000 cycles and C never did (violation rate ≤ 0.0051%, 95% upper bound), about 200 times below the 1% error budget.
  • A rare plateau of 4–42 µs operating-system stalls, not decoder speed, set the remaining risk, and no fitted distribution predicted it.
  • On calibrated replicas of IBM Heron r2 and Eagle r3 processors, the encoded circuit raised logical fidelity by 1.6–1.7 points over a bare qubit, and a decoder wait doubled the logical error after 8–13 µs.

Analysed with exact Clopper–Pearson intervals, bootstrap, Anderson–Darling and Kolmogorov–Smirnov fits, the Hill tail index and Mann–Whitney U. The circuits ran on device replicas; running them on physical IBM hardware is the next step.

LANGUAGES
Python, C
TOOLS
Qiskit 2.5, Qiskit Aer, Qiskit IBM Runtime, NumPy, SciPy, statsmodels, Matplotlib, GCC with ctypes

SKILLS

  • Quantum Error Correction
  • Qiskit
  • Statistical Analysis
  • Performance Benchmarking

C:\PROJECTS\QUANTUM\QNAS

QNASEvolutionary Discovery of Efficient Quantum Circuits

Jun 2026 – Oct 2026COMPLETEDINDEPENDENT RESEARCH

A software-only quantum neural architecture search framework: a multi-objective genetic algorithm (NSGA-II) that designs the gate layout of a variational quantum circuit for a hybrid quantum-classical classifier. It trades accuracy against circuit depth and the number of two-qubit gates, the noisiest operations on real hardware. About 3,300 lines of Python with 33 automated tests, a command-line pipeline with checkpoint and resume, and four teaching examples.

KEY RESULTS

  • On a 4-qubit image task (handwritten digits, even versus odd), the search evaluated 1,176 circuits and produced a Pareto front of 9 accuracy and cost trade-offs.
  • An evolved circuit matched the best hand-crafted template, StronglyEntanglingLayers with 3 layers (83.6% vs 83.9% test accuracy, p = 0.63), with 2 fewer two-qubit gates and 3 fewer layers of depth.
  • A pruned circuit with 4 two-qubit gates reached 77.1%, and one with a single two-qubit gate and one trainable angle reached 73.1%, both above a logistic-regression baseline (67.9%).
  • A barren-plateau scan reproduced the exponential fall of gradient variance from 2 to 10 qubits, and under 3% depolarizing noise the small evolved circuits kept 63–69% accuracy where the 8-gate template fell to chance.

Small classical networks were competitive (79–80%), so the study is about circuit architecture, cost and trainability rather than quantum advantage.

BareMetal-RTOSMulti-Threaded Real-Time Kernel

Apr 2026 – Oct 2026

A lightweight, multi-threaded real-time operating system kernel for ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers, running directly on the hardware with no operating system underneath. It schedules concurrent tasks under strict timing and processes sensor telemetry with low latency, the kind of workload found in robots, drones and industrial controllers.

FEATURES

  • A preemptive, priority-based scheduler driven by the SysTick timer, with context switching in the PendSV handler.
  • Thread control blocks with separate stacks, and sleep, yield and delay services for tasks.
  • Synchronisation primitives (mutexes, semaphores and message queues) for safe data sharing between threads and interrupts.
  • A low-latency telemetry path: sensors are read in interrupt handlers and samples reach processing tasks through lock-free ring buffers.

MateCheckReverse Chess for Analysing Your Chess Knowledge

Apr 2026 – May 2026GAME · NEW CONCEPT

A new way to test chess understanding. MateCheck starts at the end of a game, with only a king and queen on the board, and you play the game backwards. Each retro move un-moves a piece or un-captures one, bringing pawns, knights, bishops and rooks back onto the board until the armies are rebuilt. At the end, MateCheck analyses the game you reconstructed, move by move, from the opening to the final position.

FEATURES

  • A retrograde move generator that allows only legal backward moves: un-moves, un-captures that return a piece, and un-promotions.
  • Pieces unlock as you go: each correct un-capture gives you access to pawns, knights, bishops and rooks.
  • The reconstructed game is replayed forwards and every position is evaluated with Stockfish, marking strong, inaccurate and losing moves.
  • A score for how plausible and how strong your reconstruction is, plus puzzles that start from famous final positions.
LANGUAGES
TypeScript, JavaScript
TOOLS
React, chess.js, react-chessboard, Stockfish (WebAssembly), Node.js, Express, MongoDB

SKILLS

GYRO-76

A College Application Essay

Swayam Shakya

Cricket was my world from the time I was a child. Every morning, my first thought was how to give my best at that day’s practice. My body was a map of random bruises and exhaustion, and my room was filled with sportswear, Slazenger shoes, and posters. I lived for the roar of the crowd, the energy of my teammates, the intensity of the field, the sudden twists of a close match, and the celebrations afterward. I served as vice captain of my school team for two years and as an instructor for the junior team. Yet cricket was only one part of me, and I soon realized that it was not what I wanted to pursue.

That realization came midway through my final year of school. Like my classmates, I was preoccupied with grades, admissions, and board exams, while also being expected to take part in several projects and activities. Everyone joined the groups that interested them most. Unfortunately, the sports group I wanted was already full, so I decided to join the Computer Science group instead.

At first, I regretted that decision. The group had four members, including me. Our leader was an introvert, and the other two members seemed disengaged. Honestly, even a casual conversation felt like a struggle. I could not tell whether they were uncomfortable around me or simply ignoring me. For days, we gathered, sat together for hours, and left without so much as exchanging a glance. It was deeply frustrating. That began to change on the day Mr. Thapa, our instructor, arrived.

My first impression of him was skeptical. He dressed like a rowdy biker, with black sunglasses, silver chains around his neck, and a tattoo on his neck. Now this group is full of misfits, I thought. It was immature of me to judge him by his outfit. Although his methods were unconventional, he possessed exceptional skill and knowledge. His presence gradually lifted the gloomy atmosphere of the room. Our leader began to share his ideas and opinions, the girls became attentive and interactive, and I came to understand them a little better each day. I was glad. Soon afterward, we encountered an event that would have a profound impact on my life.

A neighboring school announced an Inter-School Education Fair, where students could showcase their talents in a field of their choice. Mr. Thapa gathered us in the room to discuss our project. As we entered, I noticed him sketching structures on the board. Curious, I asked him what he was drawing. “We’re going to build a robot,” he answered eagerly. We were stunned. “But is that even possible in the time we have?” I asked. “Yes, of course,” he replied with a smile.

For the next three weeks, we worked alongside Mr. Thapa, and his dedication to the project was truly inspiring. After weeks of effort, we finally built our robot. We named it GYRO-76, combining the third letter of each member’s first name. It was the first project I had ever taken part in after giving up cricket. Deep down, I felt that our meeting had been a fortunate one, and the experience deepened my passion for computers remarkably.

My curiosity to discover more about that passion has resolved many of my doubts, and I am ready to seek answers to whatever challenges lie ahead. To me, studying computer engineering is a significant step toward my career. I am confident that my determination will help me succeed in your program, and I look forward to learning from every experience and giving my best in the field I have chosen. Someday, I hope to build another GYRO-76 with a group of lovable misfits.
